How to use this when buying

Gibson necks are finished by hand, so individual guitars vary within the same family. The clearest proof is the 1959 Burst spread: across 36 originals, the 1st fret depth ranged from .807" to .921" - a huge spread even among the same model year. That is why the honest buyer checks actual measurements instead of relying on a family name alone.

Ask the seller for caliper readings at the 1st fret and 12th fret, measured over the fret, strings on. The numbers that matter are the real-world depth over the fretboard, not a spec sheet or a vague description like "chunky" or "60s slim." If the listing gives only one number, it is not enough to judge the neck.

Provenance tells you how much confidence to place in each reading. OFFICIAL = Gibson published spec; DEALER = a dealer caliper measurement of a specific guitar; OWNER = owner caliper report from a forum; BOOK = published table in a reference book; GUIDE = vintage authentication guide or dealer example; AGG = forum aggregate or estimate. OWNER and AGG figures are single examples or broad estimates, not factory specs, so they are useful but should be treated as evidence rather than official numbers.

Depth is not the whole story. Shoulder fullness changes how a neck feels at the same depth. The cross-section renderer uses a superellipse exponent (k) to illustrate this: higher k means fuller shoulders and a more rounded "baseball bat" feel, while lower k makes a neck feel slimmer even at similar measured depth. If two guitars share the same 1st-fret depth but different shoulders, the feel can still be very different.

Sources compiled from Gibson published specs, dealer measurement listings, owner caliper reports on MyLesPaul and TheGearPage forums, and the Beauty of the Burst table. Individual guitars vary, and the best shopping strategy is to compare the actual 1st and 12th fret caliper numbers on the guitar you are considering.
